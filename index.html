The difference

Cold Read is an editor. Not a rewriter.

It points at the exact weak line, explains what happens because of it, and tells you which decision still needs to be made. It will not replace your file, write your prompt, or quietly become the author of your work.

What it checks

Problems that only appear when someone else tries to use it.

Cold Read judges each project against what that project is actually trying to do. It does not demand more files, agents, or process unless the work genuinely earns them.

01

Missing responsibility

An important job, approval, or connecting step exists in the workflow—but nothing owns it.

02

Contradictory instructions

Two files tell the AI to behave differently at the same moment, leaving the outcome to chance.

03

Unobservable rules

Words like “good,” “appropriate,” or “solid enough” sound useful but give no one a finish line they can check.

04

Knowledge stuck in your head

The project works for its builder, but a stranger or fresh session would not know how to continue.

05

Unnecessary complexity

Extra agents, duplicated rules, or reference files add cost without improving the work.

F01CriticalContradiction

Client sign-off conflicts with automatic delivery.

Why it fails: the root requires sign-off while the delivery contract sends as soon as it decides the summary is ready.

Consequence: client-facing work can leave without the promised human approval.

Builder decision: decide which surface owns sending and whether approval is mandatory before that action.

F02CriticalMissing responsibility

Payment has no human approval point.

Why it fails: the project authorises a consequential financial action, but none of the seven relevant files assigns responsibility for approving it.

Consequence: a delivery decision can trigger a client charge without a person checking that it is valid.

Builder decision: decide what must be true, and who must agree, before money is allowed to move.

F03HighHandoff gap

Research does not produce a recognisable delivery input.

Why it fails: research says to save notes and pass a brief, but no accessible rule identifies the canonical artifact that delivery should recognise.

Consequence: a fresh session cannot tell which output is complete, current, or safe to continue from.

Builder decision: decide what information must cross the stage boundary and how the next task recognises it.

F04HighUnobservable rule

“Solid enough” gives research no checkable finish line.

Why it fails: nothing ties the phrase to required evidence, coverage, or a decision the research must support.

Consequence: two sessions can stop at different points and both claim to have followed the workflow.

Builder decision: define what someone must be able to observe before research is complete.

F05HighUnobservable rule

The missing-data fallback is “use your judgment and continue.”

Why it fails: the fallback gives no boundary for when uncertainty is acceptable, disclosed, or escalated.

Consequence: unsupported market claims can flow into a client deliverable as if they were verified.

Builder decision: decide how uncertainty changes the output and when the workflow must stop or ask.

F06MediumContradiction

Two files assign incompatible client tones.

Why it fails: the root demands neutral language while the dedicated reference demands warm, conversational language.

Consequence: client voice depends on whichever file a session treats as authoritative.

Builder decision: choose one canonical owner for tone and resolve the incompatible direction.

F07MediumDuplicated ownership

Run state is deliberately maintained in two root files.

Why it fails: both files own the same current-stage and next-action responsibility, so neither is canonical.

Consequence: one update can leave two plausible but different answers about where work should resume.

Builder decision: decide which single surface owns resumable state and what the other is for, if anything.

F08MediumUnproven behavior

The project claims it never sends an incomplete summary, but shows no proof.

Why it fails: this is a material “never” guarantee on client-facing behavior, yet the supplied project contains no example or test exercising the block.

Consequence: a reader cannot distinguish an enforced boundary from a sentence the workflow may ignore.

Builder decision: demonstrate the claimed refusal against an incomplete input before relying on it.

Complete architecture assessment

Necessity read

One root guide, three workflow contracts, and one shared tone reference are proportionate for this job. The project does not earn multiple agents. The second state file is excess rather than useful separation.

Ownership, routing, and handoff

Sending is governed by conflicting owners; payment approval is unowned; research-to-delivery continuity relies on an unnamed artifact; and resumable state has two owners. The missing architecture is decisions and canonical signals—not another agent.

Prioritised action plan
  1. Settle human authority over client sending and payment.
  2. Make the research-to-delivery handoff recognisable to a fresh session.
  3. Give research completion and uncertainty observable boundaries.
  4. Choose canonical owners for tone and resumable state.
  5. Demonstrate the claimed incomplete-summary refusal.
Next test

Open the project in a fresh session halfway through a run. Using only these files, ask it to identify the current stage, locate the accepted research input, decide whether sending is authorised, and explain whether billing may proceed. Then test an incomplete summary to see whether delivery actually stops.

Boundary demonstration

Not every folder is an AI system.

Cold Read is for folder-based AI systems, including ICM projects. When an ordinary engineering folder has no root instructions, AI purpose, routing, or context layer, it stops instead of inventing an architecture to criticise.

No AI-operating intent is present.

There is no root instruction file, no CLAUDE.md, no routing or context layer, no stated purpose, and nothing in the tree that establishes an AI-operating intent.

This is a folder of engineering content, not a folder-based AI system. Cold Read reviews the architecture that turns a folder into an AI-run system: routing, ownership, rules, gates, and handoffs.

With no instruction layer present at all, there is no relevant architecture to inspect. Applying those judgments to a document and code repository would invent an architecture that is not there.

What Cold Read does next Stops. No Quick/Full question. No fabricated findings.

An ordinary folder is not marked as badly designed just because it was never intended to operate an AI specialist.

Cold Read does

“This line leaves approval unowned.”

It identifies the exact place, consequence, missing decision, and—when earned—the conceptual shape a revision needs.

Cold Read does not

“Here is your corrected file.”

No replacement wording. No ready-to-paste prompt. No finished folder structure. The builder keeps the pen.
